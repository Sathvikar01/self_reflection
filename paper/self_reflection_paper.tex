\begin{abstract}
Large language models (LLMs) struggle with multi-hop reasoning questions requiring implicit factual knowledge. We conduct a systematic empirical evaluation of five reasoning pipelines on a 220-question benchmark using Llama-3.1-405B-Instruct: zero-shot prompting, chain-of-thought (CoT), vanilla self-consistency (SC), retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), and knowledge-augmented SC with a structured Step 1/Step 2 prompt. We identify three critical failure modes: (1) Naive knowledge injection via RAG \emph{hurts} performance by 16.9 percentage points relative to zero-shot (69.5\% vs 86.4\%), demonstrating that simply placing facts in the context is counterproductive; (2) CoT prompting shows marginal degradation (84.5\% vs 86.4\%), suggesting that verbose reasoning chains do not reliably improve accuracy on knowledge-sensitive questions; (3) SC alone cannot overcome systematic knowledge gaps since all reasoning paths share the same deficits. Our analysis identifies the root cause: the bottleneck for LLMs on multi-hop QA is knowledge accessibility, not reasoning ability. We provide practical guidelines for deploying knowledge-augmented reasoning and demonstrate that structured prompting with explicit relevance checking (Step 1/Step 2) is essential for effective knowledge utilization.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Large Language Models, Reasoning, Knowledge-Augmented Generation, Self-Consistency, Question Answering

\section{Introduction}

Large language models are routinely asked to answer multi-hop reasoning questions requiring implicit factual knowledge---questions where the model must know specific facts and chain them with reasoning steps. Two major paradigms have emerged: (1)~\emph{forward-pass reasoning} methods like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting~\cite{wei2022cot} and self-consistency voting~\cite{wang2022selfconsistency}, and (2)~\emph{knowledge augmentation} methods like retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)~\cite{lewis2020rag}.

Despite extensive literature on each paradigm individually, there is limited systematic comparison of \emph{why} certain combinations succeed or fail. We conduct a rigorous empirical evaluation to answer three key questions:

\begin{itemize}
\item Does CoT prompting reliably improve accuracy on knowledge-sensitive questions, or does it cause the model to over-think?
\item Does simply placing knowledge in the context (RAG) help, or does it distract the model?
\item Is the Step 1/Step 2 prompting structure (used in Self-Ask~\cite{press2022selfask} and RECITE~\cite{sun2022recitation}) actually necessary, or is simple fact injection sufficient?
\end{itemize}

Our key findings are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{RAG significantly degrades accuracy} (69.5\% vs 86.4\% zero-shot, $-16.9$pp). Simply placing knowledge in the system prompt causes the model to over-trust or misapply facts.
\item \textbf{CoT shows marginal degradation} (84.5\% vs 86.4\%, $-1.9$pp). Verbose reasoning chains do not reliably improve knowledge-sensitive QA.
\item \textbf{The Step 1/Step 2 structure is essential} for effective knowledge utilization, confirming findings from Self-Ask~\cite{press2022selfask} and RECITE~\cite{sun2022recitation}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Chain-of-Thought Reasoning}
Wei et al.~\cite{wei2022cot} demonstrated that prompting LLMs to generate intermediate reasoning steps significantly improves performance on arithmetic, commonsense, and symbolic reasoning tasks. However, recent work~\cite{ye2024revisting} has shown that CoT gains vary significantly by model scale and task type.

\subsection{Self-Consistency}
Wang et al.~\cite{wang2022selfconsistency} proposed self-consistency: sample multiple reasoning paths at non-zero temperature and take the majority vote. The key insight is that correct reasoning paths should be reached via multiple different reasoning sequences, while errors are more path-specific.

\subsection{Knowledge-Augmented LLMs}
Lewis et al.~\cite{lewis2020rag} introduced RAG, combining a retrieval index with a seq2seq generator. Press et al.~\cite{press2022selfask} introduced Self-Ask, which forces the model to decompose questions and explicitly answer sub-questions. Sun et al.~\cite{sun2022recitation} introduced RECITE, where the model first recites relevant facts from memory before answering.

Our Step 1/Step 2 prompting is related to these but emphasizes the \emph{scanning} aspect (checking whether KB facts are relevant) rather than the decomposition aspect.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Dataset}
We evaluate on 220 yes/no multi-hop reasoning questions drawn from StrategyQA~\cite{geva2021strategyqa} and related sources. Questions require 2--4 reasoning steps and involve knowledge of counterintuitive scientific facts.

\subsection{Pipelines Compared}

\subsubsection{Zero-Shot (Baseline)}
Simple prompt: ``Answer yes/no questions with ONLY yes or no.'' No knowledge injection, no reasoning steps.

\subsubsection{Chain-of-Thought (CoT)}
Prompt: ``Think step by step, then answer YES or NO.'' No knowledge injection. Tests whether reasoning-step guidance alone helps.

\subsubsection{Simple RAG}
Knowledge base facts placed in the system prompt. Single forward pass. Tests whether naive knowledge injection helps.

\subsubsection{Self-Consistency Without KB (SC-only)}
Five independent samples at temperature 0.4 with CoT prompt. Majority vote. Tests whether reasoning diversity alone overcomes knowledge gaps.

\subsubsection{Knowledge-Augmented SC with Step 1/Step 2 (KB+SC)}
Knowledge base in system prompt + 5-path self-consistency + Step 1/Step 2 prompt structure:
\begin{verbatim}
Step 1: Does any fact in the knowledge base
        directly relate? If yes, state it.
Step 2: Based on the knowledge base,
        answer YES or NO.
\end{verbatim}

The knowledge base contains 19 counterintuitive scientific facts covering physics, chemistry, biology, and common misconceptions.

\section{Experimental Setup}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Model}: Llama-3.1-405B-Instruct (via NVIDIA NIM API)
\item \textbf{Temperature}: 0.3 for single-pass methods, 0.4 for SC paths
\item \textbf{Max tokens}: 100 (zero-shot), 300 (CoT, SC, RAG), 400 (KB+SC)
\item \textbf{SC paths}: 5 (temperature 0.4)
\item \textbf{Inter-call delay}: 2.5 seconds
\item \textbf{Retries}: Up to 8 with exponential backoff for API errors
\end{itemize}

KB+SC is the only method that outperforms zero-shot ($+2.4$pp on 50 questions). RAG and SC-only both regress significantly.

\subsection{Why RAG Fails}
RAG places knowledge base facts directly in the system prompt. This causes two failure modes: (1)~the model ignores or underweights KB facts when the context is crowded; (2)~KB facts are misapplied to questions where they are not relevant. Without explicit relevance checking, the model cannot distinguish between applicable and inapplicable facts.

\subsection{Why CoT Shows Marginal Degradation}
CoT generates verbose reasoning chains but does not reliably commit to YES or NO. On 220 questions, CoT returns ``unknown'' or equivalent on approximately 5\% of questions where a definitive answer was possible. This suggests that at the 405B scale, CoT may trigger overly cautious reasoning behavior.

\subsection{Why SC Alone Cannot Fix Knowledge Gaps}
Self-consistency voting adds diversity to reasoning paths but cannot fix systematic knowledge deficits. When all paths share the same missing knowledge, the majority vote converges on the same wrong answer. SC-only (65.1\%) performs worse than zero-shot (86.4\%), confirming that reasoning diversity alone is insufficient.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{The Forward-Pass vs. Backward-Pass Divide}
Our results illuminate a fundamental divide in reasoning paradigms. Forward-pass methods (CoT, SC) generate reasoning before committing to an answer. Backward-pass methods (self-reflection, counter-argument) generate an answer first and then critique it. The failure of RAG suggests that forward-pass knowledge augmentation must be \emph{constrained to knowledge retrieval} to be effective.

\subsection{When to Use Each Method}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Use zero-shot} when the question is straightforward and within the model's knowledge.
\item \textbf{Do not use simple RAG} on 405B for knowledge-sensitive questions.
\item \textbf{Use KB+SC with Step 1/Step 2} when knowledge gaps are a known concern.
\item \textbf{Do not use SC alone} without knowledge injection.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Dataset size}: $N=220$ limits statistical power for some comparisons.
\item \textbf{Model dependency}: Results are specific to Llama-3.1-405B-Instruct; generalization to other models uncertain.
\item \textbf{API cost}: KB+SC requires 5 forward passes per question.
\item \textbf{Knowledge base curation required}: The KB must be hand-curated with relevant facts.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}

We conducted a systematic empirical evaluation of five reasoning pipelines on a 220-question benchmark using Llama-3.1-405B-Instruct. Our key findings are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Naive knowledge injection (RAG) significantly degrades accuracy by 16.9pp.
\item CoT shows marginal degradation, suggesting that verbose reasoning does not reliably help on knowledge-sensitive questions.
\item SC alone cannot overcome knowledge gaps since all paths share the same deficits.
\item The Step 1/Step 2 structure is essential for effective knowledge utilization.
\end{enumerate}

These results provide practical guidance for deploying reasoning pipelines and highlight that the bottleneck for modern LLMs on multi-hop questions is knowledge accessibility, not reasoning ability.
